# ADDISON — Story Bible

**Committed direction:** you live here, and so did the last guy.

Everything below is delivered as memos, pay stubs, found artifacts, and barks. No cutscenes, no dialogue trees, nothing that stops the inspection loop. The four-second rule holds throughout.

---

## Premise

You are the Compliance Inspector for the Addison Master Community Association. You live at **2118 Eyezetta**. Your street is on the Friday route. The Association does not consider that a conflict.

Your predecessor also lived in the community. Nobody will discuss him. His initials are in the binder.

---

## The opening (Day 1 memo)

> Welcome to the Addison Master Community Association. You are the Compliance Inspector. There is one.
> You're at 2118 Eyezetta, yes? Good. Short commute.
> Your predecessor also lived in the community. We won't be discussing that.
> Your street is on the Friday route. We don't consider that a conflict.
> Three errors ends your day. Three reprimands ends the arrangement.
> Questions are addressed in the binder.

## What happened to R. Mercer

*The player never reads this. It's assembled from artifacts.*

Mercer was good — 94% accuracy over fourteen months. He lived at 2114 Eyezetta, four doors from you.

In his last month he cited Denton Wicks's boat under R-204. The citation was appealed and granted on a backdated variance. Then Mercer's own property received nine citations in six weeks, all written by a substitute inspector, all in Marla Hoyt's handwriting. Escalation, lien, foreclosure. He turned in his clipboard.

The Association's records say the position became available.

**The thesis line, spoken by a neighbor on Day 16:**
> They didn't run him out. They just enforced.

That's the whole game. Selective enforcement isn't corruption — it's the rules applied honestly to one person and loosely to everyone else.

## The board

Five members, roughly sixty lines total. Act III's split doesn't work without them.

| Member | Role | Function | Voice |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Denton Wicks** | President | Owns the boat. Never threatens explicitly. | Genial, uses your first name |
| **Marla Hoyt** | Secretary | Writes everything. Wrote Mercer's citations. | Procedural, passive voice |
| **Gil Abernathy** | Treasurer | Appeals cost money. Wants you *accurate*, not harsh. | Blunt, numbers only |
| **Sunny Rho** | Member | Elected on reform. Increasingly ineffective. | Earnest, apologetic |
| **Vaughn Pike** | Member | Twelve years. Says almost nothing. Votes with Wicks. | Silence, then one line |

**Act III split:** Abernathy and Rho against Wicks and Hoyt. Pike decides on Day 16 and it's the first time he's spoken.

## Endings

Determined by cumulative record, not a final choice.

| Ending | Condition |
|---|---|
| **Benchmark** | High accuracy, cited the boat, kept the job. You are what Mercer was, at the point where it stopped working. |
| **The Position Became Available** | Cited the boat, three reprimands. Same door he went out. |
| **Good Standing** | High accuracy, never cited the boat. You keep the house. Everyone knows why. |
| **Elected** | Enforced unevenly to build leverage. You're on the board. Wicks likes you. |
| **Filed** | Compiled the selective-enforcement record and took it outside the Association. |
| **Moved** | Quit, or your own lien completed. Addison continues. |

No ending is labeled correct. The pay stub is the last thing you read in every one of them.

## The pay stub

One line under the numbers, every day. Cheapest story vehicle in the game because it's attached to money.

- *Adjustment: appeal costs, 1 case.*
- *Note: your route was changed at a board member's request.*
- *Reminder: dues are collected from employees as well.*
- *Note: your bins were observed at the curb Thursday.*
- *Deduction: association legal fees, prorated.*
- *Your accuracy this period: 71%. Board review is Thursday.*
- *No adjustment. Thank you for your service to the community.*

---

## Writing rules, restated

1. The Association uses passive voice. It never says who decided.
2. Residents speak in specifics. That gap is the argument.
3. Nothing over twenty words. Memos are three to four lines.
4. Nobody comments that the rules are absurd.
5. Backstory is found, never told.
6. **Mercer never appears.** No flashback, no letter to the player, no voice. He exists only in handwriting, records, and what neighbors won't say. The moment he speaks directly, the register breaks.
