# ADDISON — NPC Barks

Ambient word-bubble lines, triggered when the inspector is on screen. ~120 lines.

**One design note before the list:** some of these should leak information. *"The pod's leaving Thursday — it's been leaving Thursday for a month"* tells the player the pod has exceeded its permit window. *"It's dormant, not dead"* flags the decoy. That makes barks functional rather than decorative, and it rewards players who read them. Tag those separately so you can weight them onto lots where the tell matters.

Tone key: 🟢 lighthearted · ⚪ neutral · 🔴 upset

# Part 2 — Passers-by on the sidewalk

Different speaker, different rules. Homeowners are defending property. Passers-by have **no stake and no accountability** — they're just walking, and they'll say anything.

**The mechanic that matters here:** homeowner barks leak *truth*. Passer-by barks leak *opinion*, and they should sometimes be wrong. A neighbor saying "that needs mowing" on a lawn that measures 5 inches is a false lead. That teaches the player to trust the rulebook over social pressure — which is the entire thesis of the game, delivered for free through ambient dialogue.

## Implementation notes

**Structure.** Each line wants `{ id, pool, tone, text, requires }` — where `requires` gates on lot violation state, citation history, weekday, or temperature.

**Weighting.** Roughly 50% generic and mundane, 25% defensive on violating lots, 15% grievance and snitching, 10% quiet. Push the ratio toward grievance and quiet as the campaign darkens.

**No repeats within a day.** Barks that recur inside one session kill the effect faster than bad writing does.

**Escalate by act.** Act I leans lighthearted. By Act III the same character types should be mostly upset and quiet — reuse the pools, change the weights. Free tonal progression.

**Barks as evidence.** The self-incriminating pool is the interesting one. A player who reads bubbles gets a real edge on grace-period and decoy cases, which rewards attention without adding UI.

## Passer-by specifics

**Reliability is inverted.** Homeowners tell the truth accidentally. Passers-by state opinions confidently and are sometimes wrong. Fire "that needs mowing" on compliant lawns deliberately — a player who cites on social pressure instead of measuring gets a strike, and learns the lesson the game is actually about.

**Gate on features, not just violations.** The shutters line needs shutters. The paint line needs a repaint since the player's last visit. Ungated cosmetic critique of a feature that isn't there reads as a bug.

**Bubbles must be short.** These speakers are moving. Anything over about seven words gets clipped as they exit frame — the fragments and half-conversations are written short for exactly this reason.

**Pairs and props.** Walking companions need two NPCs pathing together; the dog-walker pool needs a leashed dog. Both are worth building — a sidewalk with only solo walkers reads as depopulated.

**Repeat traffic is a feature.** "I'm going to walk past again" should be literal. Same NPC, second loop, different line.
